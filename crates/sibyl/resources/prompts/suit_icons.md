Authoritative suit-iconography rules. These are defined by the application (not the owner)
and constrain how every suit icon is generated, regardless of the owner's chosen deck style.
They take precedence over the style direction on any conflict.

- Produce exactly four suit icons: Cups, Wands, Swords, and Pentacles.
- Each icon is a single, centered emblem of its suit's classical object: a chalice/cup, a
  wooden wand/staff, a blade/sword, and a coin/pentacle disc.
- Keep one consistent visual language across all four — matching line weight, level of
  detail, perspective, and framing — so the set reads unmistakably as one deck.
- Icons must stay legible at small sizes and as silhouettes; avoid fine clutter.
- No text, letters, numerals, or signatures anywhere in an icon.
- No human faces or figures in the suit icons (figures belong on the card faces).
- Symmetric, emblem-like composition centered in a square, on a simple or transparent
  background so the icon composites cleanly onto cards.
- Honor traditional tarot symbolism — water/emotion for Cups, fire/will for Wands,
  air/intellect for Swords, earth/material for Pentacles — expressed through the owner's
  chosen style.
